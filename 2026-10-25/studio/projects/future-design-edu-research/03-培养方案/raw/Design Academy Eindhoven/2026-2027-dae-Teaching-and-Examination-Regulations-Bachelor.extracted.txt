Article 1
 General provisions and definitions
1.
This regulation is a TER as referred to in Article 7.13 WHW.
a.
The provisions contained in these regulations are only legally valid if and 
insofar as they do not conflict with stipulations made in law or pursuant to 
Dutch law; 
b.
The regulations are adopted by DAE’s Executive Board with the consent of 
the BCC and in consultation with the Examination Board.
2.
The scope of these regulations is restricted to students at DAE who fall under 
the jurisdiction of DAE’s Executive Board.
a.
The Executive Board is responsible for regular assessment of the TER, for 
the purpose of monitoring and, where necessary, adjusting the study 
load. The Executive Board considers the time students are required to 
spend on the education as a result of the study load.
3.
In accordance with Section 7.13 of the WHW, the TER is part of this Student 
Charter and is available on DAE’s online platforms.
4.
In this chapter, the following additional definitions apply: 
a.
Programme of study: the full-time Bachelor degree programme in Design 
(B Design – CROHO 39111) offered by DAE;
b.
Propaedeutic phase: first phase of the programme of study, namely the 
first year of the Bachelor degree programme (60 EC), as referred to in 
Section 7.8 of the WHW. In this phase, undergraduate students gain an 
understanding of the substance of the programme and the intended 
profession. Selection and referral are possible at the end of this phase 
(Section 7.8(5) of the WHW). This phase concludes with the propaedeutic 
examination (Section 7.8(3) and (4) of the WHW);
c.
Main phase: The main phase comprises the second year, the third year, 
and the first semester of the fourth year of the Bachelor programme. This 
phase follows the propaedeutic phase of the Bachelor programme and 
comprises 150 EC. 
d.
Examination phase: The examination phase is the period during which the 
course units Graduation 4B (final examination) and Reflections 4B are 
offered to the student. This phase comprises 30 EC.
5.
The education, assessment and examination are conducted in English. The 
specific nature of the field of design and the background of the student body 
require this, in accordance with the Code of Conduct for the International 
Student adopted by DAE’s Executive Board.
Article 2
 Exemptions for Bachelor programme
Following consultation with the head of the Bachelor programme, the Examination 
Board is authorised to grant exemptions to students who have acquired certain 
skills through study or work experience before being admitted to DAE. These skills 
have present qualities equal to the learning outcomes and criteria at DAE. 
Article 3
 Structure of Bachelor programme
1.
The education in the Bachelor programme consists of a propaedeutic phase 
and the main phase. 
2.
The Bachelor programme consists of a total of 240 EC: 60 EC for the 
propaedeutic phase, 150 for the main phase, and 30 for the final examination. 
3.
The Bachelor programme consists of the following units of learning:
a. Essentials (10 EC) is the preparatory module in which students familiarise 
themselves with the various workshops and techniques available at DAE 
and the various Studios.
b. Fundamentals (55 EC) is the core module in which students gather the 
fundamental knowledge, skills, insights and tools of design. 
c. Studios (60 EC) is the core module in which students are given the 
opportunity to discover different aspects, research and practices in design 
by choosing one of the several studios at DAE.

Article 4
 Propaedeutic phase of Bachelor programme
1.
The propaedeutic phase of the Bachelor programme consists of the course 
components: Fundamentals 1A, Fundamentals 1B, Studio 1B, Essentials 1A and 
Reflections 1A and 1B, which account for a total of 60 EC. 
2.
The qualifications to pass the propaedeutic phase consist of the learning 
outcomes of the course components mentioned in (1). 
3.
To pass the propaedeutic phase, the student must meet the standard for the 
propaedeutic phases as specified in the EC table in Annex I: they must attain 60 
EC. Thus, the propaedeutic standard is as follows: all propaedeutic course 
components (Fundamentals 1A, Fundamentals 1B and Studio 1B) must have 
been assessed as ‘sufficient’, ‘good’ or, in the case of Essentials 1A and 
Reflections 1A and 1B, ‘yes’.
4.
The propaedeutic standard requires the successful completion of at least 45 EC 
in the first year of study and at least 60 EC within two years of commencement 
of the programme. 
5.
At the end of the first year of enrolment, or for as long as the propaedeutic 
standard has not yet been met, the Executive Board issues each student a 
binding study advice regarding the student’s continuation within the 
programme of study. 
6.
If the propaedeutic standard referred to in (4) is not met, a negative binding 
study recommendation (BSA) will automatically be issued.
7.
A student who passes the propaedeutic examination will receive a certificate 
from the Examination Board as proof thereof.
8.
Propaedeutic students receive advice twice regarding the progress of their 
studies in relation to a negative binding study recommendation. The second 
recommendation may coincide with the study recommendation as referred to in 
Section 7.8b of the WHW. Students with a delay also receive advice at the start or 
change of their individual study scenario. 
9.
The Executive Board may attach a rejection to the negative binding study 
recommendation as referred to in the previous paragraph of this article. This 
happens if the student has not yet completed the propaedeutic phase within two 
years of starting the programme and is not deemed suitable for the 
programme based on the student’s study results. Before the Executive Board 
issues a rejection, the student is given a warning with a reasonable time limit to 
improve the student’s study results.
10. Students who have received a negative binding study recommendation that 
includes a rejection are no longer eligible to enrol at DAE in the same 
programme of study.

Article 5
 Main phase of Bachelor programme
1.
The main phase of the Bachelor programme consists of Fundamentals 2A, 2B, 
3A, Studio’s 2A, 2B, 3A, Internship 3B, Focus 4A, Reflections 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B, and 
4A, which account for 150 EC. An overview of the EC’s is available each year and 
can be viewed in Annex I of the Student Charter. 
2.
Students take part in assessment for the course components listed in (1) in the 
order listed in the EC-table in Appendix 1. Individual study scenarios have been 
drawn up for the cohorts who started in 2021 or earlier; all students in these 
cohorts have been assigned scenarios in accordance with the applicable 
conversion. 
3.
Deviations from the standard order of course components of the programme of 
study are granted according to the existing scenarios provided in Appendix 2; 
any deviations from this standard order must take the form of an individual 
study scenario, which must be requested from the Examination Board.
4.
The main phase is concluded once all course components referred to in (1) 
have been successfully completed. 
Article 6
 Assessments of Bachelor programme
1.
Every course component is concluded by assessment. During assessment, the 
knowledge, skills and insights of the student are evaluated for the learning 
outcome of that specific course component.
2.
Within the Bachelor programme, DAE works with learning outcomes, which the 
student must have demonstrated at the time of assessment of a specific unit of 
learning. Based on this evaluation of the learning outcomes, the assessment 
determines if a student passes that unit of learning. 
3.
The assessment is set up by the tutor or tutor team of each course component 
to match the learning outcomes and teaching activities. The tutor or tutor team 
is responsible for the assessing, grading and feedback during assessment.  The 
coordinator of each course component is responsible for the logistics of 
organising the assessment within the frameworks of DAE policy. 
4.
To be assessed, one must be enrolled as a student. 
5.
Students are offered at least two opportunities per academic year to sit 
assessments for the course components in the propaedeutic, main and 
examination phase. 
6.
The first opportunity is the standard assessment, which takes place at the end 
of the course component. The second opportunity is called the ‘redo’ and takes 
place during the redo week which is a predetermined week in the Calendarium. 
7.
Students who do not attend an assessment opportunity are considered to have 
forfeited their opportunity for assessment. The assessment will be evaluated with 
a Not Present (NP).
8.
All assessments and examinations of course components within the Bachelor 
programme are taken by the student individually, either in written or oral form

12. In case of illness or absence during assessment, the student will notify the 
coordinator of the course component, by 9:30 on the day of assessment at the

will take part in the next available opportunity for assessment. 
13. The right to a redo is only applicable if:
a.
the standard assessment opportunity was not passed;
b.
the student was not present during standard assessment opportunity and 
received an NP, regardless of the reason. 
14. If there are no more opportunities for assessment, the course component needs 
to be completed fully when the course component is next taught in the 
programme of study. 
15. As a rule, oral evaluations and assessments are not open to the public; in 
exceptional cases, the Examination Board or the Committee of Examiners in 
question may decide to deviate from this rule. It is always prohibited to record 
assessments.
16. If a student either misses or fails both the first and second assessment 
opportunities, they can submit a request to the Examination Board for an 
additional (third) assessment opportunity (extra assessment) after receiving the 
results. This request must be submitted in writing within 10 working days of 
receiving the results.
17. An extra assessment opportunity is only granted in cases of exceptional 
personal circumstances, which must be supported by documentation and 
known to the student counsellor. The student counsellor can help the student 
with the request process and contact with the Examination Board. 
18. When deciding on an extra assessment, additional conditions may be imposed, 
such as achieving a minimum percentage of completed assignments or 
meeting attendance requirements for the course.
19. The third assessment opportunity is offered twice per academic year and is 
included in the Calendarium.
20. The decision of the Examination Board is final and will be communicated to the 
student in writing.
21. The results of assessment are visible to the student via the OSIRIS student 
following system within 10 working days of the assessment taking place, and no 
later than the day before redo week as determined by the Calendarium. 
22. The statutory validity of the successfully completed assessments is 10 years, 
calculated from the day on which the result in question was achieved. Students 
who start the programme later, or who resume their studies following an 
absence, are assigned an amended study programme by the Examination 
Board, taking into consideration the course components the student has 
already passed and the requirements of the current study programme.

Article 7 
End qualifications of Bachelor programme
The end qualifications of the final examination of the Bachelor programme form the 
framework to assure the qualities of the graduates of DAE. The end qualifications 
are formulated in five categories, as follows.
1.
Creation
a.
Creation: The student is able to initiate and sustain an creative design 
process in which conceptual approaches and material execution are 
closely intertwined to translate ideas into form through a deep 
understanding of medium, material, and techniques.
b.
Experimentation: The student is able to investigate both traditional and 
emerging technologies and methods, working with uncertainty and using 
making as a mode of thinking in which sensory experience and formal 
knowledge are inseparable.
c.
Curiosity: The student is able to continuously question their own methods, 
formal decisions, and outcomes not only individually, but through 
interaction with peers and the world.
2.
Research
a.
Inquiry: The student is able to apply creative research as an iterative, 
generative and collective component of design processes, in which 
making, thinking, and critical inquiry are inseparable and mutually 
reinforcing.
b.
Documentation: The student is able articulate and document creative 
research trajectories in ways that make the relationship between process, 
decisions, and outcomes legible.
c.
Translation: The student is able to translate creative research methods 
and outcomes into materialized projects that communicate these to 
relevant audiences.
3.
Reflection
a.
Reflection: The student is able to reflect critically on their own work, 
development, and process: not only from an individual standpoint, but 
through sustained exchange with peers, tutors, and the broader field.
b.
Perspective: The student is able to clarify and develop their perspective 
and position as a designer, building a continuously evolving, research-
informed practice.
c.
Purpose: The student is able to reflect on their practice as part of larger 
social, cultural, and ecological system and allow this awareness to 
purposefully inform their creative and professional decisions.
4.
Context
a.
Contextualisation: The student is able to critically situate their work within 
relevant theoretical, historical, and contemporary contexts and their 
societal, ethical, ecological, and political dimensions.
b.
Relationality: The student is able to engage with diverse social, cultural, 
and ecological systems by building relationships with different 
communities, knowledge systems and ways of knowing.

c.
Impact: The student is able to act as a catalyst for change through their 
practice and take responsibility for the consequences of their work and 
identifying where design can contribute to transitions towards more 
equitable, sustainable, and regenerative conditions.
5.
Profession
a.
Positioning: The student is able to communicate and position their work 
clearly, developing a design practice and engaging it meaningfully with 
diverse audiences and stakeholders.
b.
Autonomy: The student is able to work with autonomy in a professional 
role, collaborative format, and working condition that matches their work 
and evolving identity of practice.
c.
Continuity: The student is able to sustain and develop a practice beyond 
the programme drawing on their knowledge, methods, and network to 
actively contribute to and continuously develop in the field.
Article 8 
Graduation phase of Bachelor programme
1.
The Bachelor programme concludes with the graduation phase (30 EC). This 
includes the course components Graduation 4B (25 EC) and Reflections 4B (5 
EC). This graduation phase ends with the final examination of the Bachelor 
programme. 
2.
The graduation phase of the Bachelor programme is completed when all 
assessments and the final examination of the Bachelor programme have been 
completed with sufficient scores, unless the Examination Board has determined 
that research is to be conducted with regard to the final examination.
3.
Only students in the Bachelor programme who have gained 210 EC (60 EC in the 
propaedeutic phase and 150 EC in the main phase) can be admitted to the 
graduation phase, in which they are offered the opportunity to take the final 
examination. Students are required to register for and take the final 
examination at the first available opportunity once they have completed all the 
course components of the propaedeutic and main phases.
4.
The start and end dates of the graduation phase are determined by the 
Executive Board after recommendation from the Examination Board. The week 
in which students will sit the final examination must be announced at least five 
months before the date of the exam. The exact date of the final examination 
must be announced at least one month before the date of the examination. 
5.
During the graduation phase, the student is required to be supervised by a tutor 
who acts as the main guiding tutor. In addition, the student may request 
additional supervision from another tutor within DAE, as described in the 
Manual of Graduation .
6.
Participation in the midterm of course component Graduation 4B is a condition 
to be able to participate in the final examination. 
7.
The final examination assesses the Graduation 4B course component as a 
whole. During the graduation phase, during the scheduled hours and guidance 
hours, the candidate must inform the main guiding tutor of the progress of the 
candidate’s studies and final projects. The Executive Board is authorised to 
exclude students from sitting the final examination if they do not comply with 
this requirement. 
8.
Students graduate with two separate graduation projects. Graduation projects 
consist of:
a.
the creation of a work and the execution of research that relates to the 
course components followed by the student;
b.
the production of the Deliverables of Graduation as described in the 
Manual of Graduation 4B.
9.
Prior to the start of the semester, the student and the main guiding tutor must 
reach agreement on the plans for the graduation assignments.
10. The candidate is not permitted to enter into cooperation agreements or 
contracts with third parties for graduation projects without written permission 
from the Executive Board.

11.
Only students with written permission from the Examination Board may deviate 
from the number of graduation projects. 
12. Students who wish to complete one of the graduation projects in collaboration 
with one other candidate, and to use this project to graduate, must obtain 
written permission from the Examination Board. The Manual of Graduation 4B 
contains guidelines on this matter. 
13. Candidates who stop working on the Graduation 4B course component before it 
is completed must, at the first available opportunity, restart the component with 
two new graduation projects.
14. The assessment of the graduation projects is performed by the Committee of 
Examiners. 
a.
The chair of the Committee of Examiners is authorised to require 
candidates to explain their graduation projects during the examination, 
for no longer than half an hour. 
b.
When assessing the results of the graduation projects, the Committee of 
Examiners assesses alignment with the learning outcomes.
c.
A valid assessment of the projects is only possible if the portfolio and 
processlog are available to the Committee of Examiners. 
d.
Each project is assessed by means of feedback and a mark per project. 
Only entire marks are to be given for individual projects. If a project is 
assessed with grade 5 or lower, a redo of that project will follow. If one or 
two projects are to be redone, the examination is not passed, and no final 
mark is calculated. When both projects have been passed (assessed with 
a grade 6,0 or higher) a final assessment follows in the form of a single 
mark for the final examination, being the average of scores for both 
projects. This total average may contain half marks. The final examination 
is passed when this average is at least a 6,0. 
e.
For each graduation project, the assessment is documented on an 
assessment form. The assessment form includes:

 the evaluation of the assessed learning outcomes expressed in 
feedback on the learning outcomes;

a project mark in whole marks;

the total average of the final examination (the grade between 1 and 
10 that is the average of the two projects; half marks are allowed for 
the final mark);

in case of a redo, the form includes the description of the redo 
assignment based on feedback that describes improvements to be 
made with regards to the learning outcomes.
f.
The assessment per project, and the final assessment of Graduation 4B, 
are thus expressed on a scale of 1 to 10. The numbers have the following 
meanings:
1 = poor 
6 = sufficient 
2 = very weak 
7 = more than sufficient 
3 = weak 
8 = good 
4 = very insufficient 
9 = very good 
5 = insufficient 
10 = excellent 
15. The DAE degree certificate is awarded if the final mark for Graduation 4B is at 
least 6,0 and if the candidate has successfully completed all other course 
components of the Bachelor programme (see Appendix 1).
16. Whether or not a candidate has passed is confirmed by meeting of the 
Committee of Examiners. The candidate is informed of the result as soon as 
possible following the meeting.

Board.
2.

The current revision enters into force on 1 September 2026. These regulations

with the consent of the IPC and the BCC and following consultation with the 
Examination Board.
